Avni Mittal

My research is driven by the goal of building interpretable and reliable AI systems that enable efficient and trustworthy reasoning in high-stakes settings. Although large language models have demonstrated remarkable performance, their internal mechanisms remain poorly understood, leaving them vulnerable to hallucinations, brittle reasoning, and unexpected failures.

I am particularly interested in understanding how reasoning emerges inside models and how this understanding can be used to design systems whose behavior is robust, faithful, and aligned. Concretely, my work focuses on two complementary directions:

  • Mechanistic transparency: studying internal representations and architectural components to reveal latent structure, diagnose reasoning failures, and develop principled methods for steering model behavior.
  • Agentic reliability: designing evaluation frameworks for reasoning faithfulness and exploring verifiable alignment strategies that enforce safety in agent-based and multi-agent systems.

My interest in these questions has been shaped by diverse research experiences spanning inherently interpretable Neural Cellular Automata and the orchestration of large language model based agents. These experiences have highlighted that reliability must be engineered both at the level of internal representations and at the level of system design.

More broadly, I am motivated by the challenge of bridging high-level agentic behavior with low-level mechanistic understanding of how models represent and manipulate knowledge. In the long term, I aim to pursue research at the intersection of interpretability, reinforcement learning, and trustworthy agentic systems, addressing emerging challenges such as misalignment, deceptive reasoning, and failure modes that arise in complex multi-agent environments.
